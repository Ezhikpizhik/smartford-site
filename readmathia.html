Two Token Types, Three Missions

Start with Yellow Tokens for younger kids, add Pink Tokens as they level up. Then choose your mission.

Yellow Tokens · Ages 3–5

Short Words & Simple Math

Yellow tokens feature short 3–4 letter words and simple addition and subtraction with sums and differences up to 10. Perfect for early readers and young counters who are just building their first skills — visual, fast, and confidence-building.

3–4 letter words Picture matching Math up to 10 Ages 3–5
Pink Tokens · Ages 6–7+

Longer Words & Advanced Math

Pink tokens feature longer 5–7 letter words and more challenging addition and subtraction with sums and differences up to 20. Kids who have mastered the basics can mix Yellow and Pink tokens for an extra challenge.

5–7 letter words Word reading Math up to 20 Ages 6–7+
Mission A

The Reading Track

Three levels: match pictures (under 4), read a word and find the picture (ages 4–5), or look at a picture and find the matching word (ages 6–7).

Mission B

The Math Track

Solve the expression on your token (e.g. 1+5) and race to find the token on the table showing the answer (6). Yellow tokens go up to 10, Pink up to 20.

Mission C · Ages 5+

The Battle Board

A logic duel! Place tokens into two number sentences on the board, checking <, >, and = after every move. When both sentences are true, shout "READMATHIA!" to win.

The Science Behind It

ReadMathia is designed around how children actually learn to read and count.

Reading Through Repetition

Children learn to read by seeing words repeatedly in meaningful contexts. Every card in ReadMathia pairs a word with a picture, reinforcing the connection between print and meaning in the same way early literacy research recommends — active, repeated exposure that feels like play.

Number Sense Develops Through Comparison

Research on early math shows that comparing quantities — "more," "less," "equal" — is foundational to number sense and later arithmetic. ReadMathia makes comparison the core mechanic of every round, so kids build this critical skill through every card they play.

Speed Builds Fluency

The race element isn't just fun — it's functional. Timed practice (even informal, game-based pacing) is one of the most reliable ways to build reading fluency and automatic number recall. Kids who can read and count quickly have more mental resources for comprehension and problem-solving.

Games Outperform Worksheets

A University of Oregon meta-analysis of 18 studies found a 76% chance that number board games improve children's numeracy. Game-based practice produces stronger and longer-lasting skill gains than passive drill — because engagement is the engine of learning.
